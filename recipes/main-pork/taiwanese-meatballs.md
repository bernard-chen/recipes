# Taiwanese meatballs

## Ingredients
* 1.5 lbs 80/20 ground pork
* 0.5 lbs shrimp, peeled and deveined
* 10 leaves of napa cabbage
* 1 T ginger, grated or chopped finely
* 1 T ginger, cut into thick slices and smashed
* 3 scallions, cut into 1.5 inch pieces
* 6 cups chicken stock
* sesame oil
* 2 T soy sauce
* salt
* rice

## Directions
* Start the rice cooking in the rice cooker.
* Mince the napa cabbage into small pieces. It should be about six cups, chopped. Place in a large mixing bowl and add salt and sesame oil. Mix so that the chopped cabbage is coated.
* Mince the shrimp finely. Mix the shrimp, grated ginger, and ground pork into the napa cabbage.
* In a wide, but somewhat deep pan, heat some sesame oil and add the sliced ginger and scallions. When they become fragrant, add chicken stock and soy sauce. The chicken stock should be deep enough that the meatballs will float, but they won't need to be submerged.
* Once the stock comes to a boil, reduce heat to medium. Make meatballs by dipping a spoon into the boiling liquid, then using the spoon and one hand to form each ball. Use the spoon to place the meatball into the boiling liquid, thereby cleaning off the spoon again.
* Let the meatballs simmer for 15 minutes. Serve over rice with some of the broth.

## Notes
* The recipe can be reduced, but try to keep the proportions roughly the same: 3:1 ratio of pork to shrimp and as much napa cabbage as can be bound by the meat. The current recipe will not fit in one 12-inch pan.
* 80/20 ground pork means that it is 80% lean, 20% fat. A lower percentage of fat will generally produce a drier meatball. A traditional recipe may have 30% or more fat.
* The meatballs will not be fully submerged. If they are floating, they should turn over as they cook so they don't end up with a raw portion on top. They may need to be encouraged to flip partway through the cooking.
* It's helpful to cut a meatball in half to see if it is fully cooked. It may take less than the 15 minutes of simmering, and it's better to not overcook the meatballs. The cooking time depends on the size of the meatballs.
* This recipe is a variation of "lion's head meatballs". A traditional recipe would not have the shrimp or napa cabbage. It might have water chestnuts or shiitake. It might also be seasoned with oyster sauce, soy sauce, white pepper, five-spice powder, and then to bind the wet ingredients, some breadcrumbs, cornstarch, and an egg.
